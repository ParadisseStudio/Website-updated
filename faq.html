Manicures & pedicures

What is a Russian manicure, and do you offer it in Richmond Hill?

Yes, it is our specialty. A Russian (dry) manicure skips the soak and uses fine cuticle scissors and nippers for meticulous cuticle work, so the gel sits flush against the cuticle for a cleaner look that grows out beautifully and lasts longer. Angely and Sofiia are both trained in this technique. See nail services and prices.

A gel manicure is $55, a regular polish manicure is $25 and a gel French manicure is $60. Gel manicure with nail art starts at $60 and gel extensions are $65. Every manicure includes shaping, cuticle care, a cream massage and a warm towel wrap. View the full nail menu.

How much does a pedicure cost?

A regular polish pedicure is $50 and a gel pedicure is $60, both with a warm soak, exfoliating scrub, electric callus care, cream massage and warm towel wrap. Quick polish changes are $25 for regular and $30 for gel. Manicure and pedicure combos run from $75 to $120. See pedicure options.

How long does a gel manicure last?

With careful prep and good aftercare, a gel manicure from Paradisse Studio typically lasts two to three weeks without chipping. Daily cuticle oil and gloves for dishes help it last even longer. Read our gel aftercare tips.

Gel or regular polish: which is better for my nails?

Both are healthy when applied and removed properly. Gel lasts two to three weeks with a glossy, chip-resistant finish; regular polish is quick to change and removes in seconds. Many clients alternate between the two. Read our honest comparison.

Do you do nail art, chrome and French tips?

Yes. Our lightweight gel extensions are $65, shaped and blended to your hands with a high-gloss finish, cream massage and hot towel wrap included. Everything to know about gel extensions.

How should I remove gel polish without damaging my nails?

Never peel it off. Gently buff the shiny top layer, soak with acetone-saturated cotton wrapped in foil for 10 to 15 minutes, then ease the softened gel away with a wooden stick. Or simply book a removal with us. Step-by-step guide.

How often should I get a manicure?

Gel clients usually return every two to three weeks; regular polish clients every one to two weeks. Even without polish, a monthly manicure keeps cuticles healthy and nails conditioned. Find your rhythm.

What is the difference between a gel manicure and gel extensions?

A gel manicure ($55) applies gel polish to your natural nails. Gel extensions ($65) build lightweight, durable length and shape on top of your nails, then finish with gel. Extensions are ideal if your nails struggle to grow long. Learn about gel extensions.

Do you offer a gel French manicure?

Can I get just a polish change?

Is a Russian manicure safe for my nails?

Yes, when it is done by a trained technician. The technique removes only the dead cuticle tissue with precise tools and never forces or over-cuts the living skin, which is why Angely and Sofiia trained specifically in it. Why cuticles matter.

How should I prepare for my manicure appointment?

Arrive with clean, polish-free nails if you can, skip cutting your cuticles at home, and bring any inspiration photos. If you are wearing gel from elsewhere, book a removal with your service. Prep tips for a flawless manicure.

Which nail shape suits my hands?

Round and squoval are low-maintenance and hard to break; almond and oval elongate the fingers; square and coffin make a statement and usually work best on extensions. We will help you choose in the chair. Find your shape.

Nail health

Why do my nails peel, and how can I fix it?

Peeling usually comes from dryness, harsh removal or filing back and forth. Daily cuticle oil, gloves for wet work and gentle one-direction filing let healthy new growth come in. Read the full guide.

How can I strengthen weak, brittle nails?

Protect them from water and chemicals, moisturize daily with cuticle oil and hand cream, file gently and give them time; new growth takes two to three months to show. Our strengthening routine.

How do I take care of my nails at home between visits?

Keep it simple: mild soap, dry hands fully, cuticle oil nightly, gloves for dishes and never use your nails as tools. Beginner's guide and between-visit care.

How do I protect my nails in a Richmond Hill winter?

Cold, dry air is a nail's biggest seasonal challenge. Hydrate more than you think you need, wear gloves outside every time and keep your length sensible until spring. Winter nail care tips.

What do white spots or ridges on my nails mean?

White spots are usually minor knocks from weeks ago and grow out harmlessly. Vertical ridges are a normal part of getting older. Neither needs treatment, but a professional manicure keeps them looking smooth. Understanding nail ridges and seven signs your nails need care.

How do I care for my nails after removing acrylics or gel?

Expect them to feel thin at first. Hydrate relentlessly with cuticle oil twice a day, take a short polish break if you can, keep nails short while they recover and file gently in one direction. Our recovery plan.

Why is cuticle oil so important?

Cuticle oil keeps the nail plate and surrounding skin flexible and hydrated, which prevents peeling, hangnails and breakage and helps gel last longer. Once or twice a day makes a visible difference within weeks. Why cuticle oil is the secret.

Which foods help nails grow stronger?

Protein, biotin-rich foods like eggs, nuts and sweet potatoes, iron from leafy greens and lentils, and zinc from seeds all support stronger nails over time. Consistency matters more than any single food. The best foods for nails.

How can I keep nail art looking fresh?

Apply cuticle oil daily but wipe it off the design, wear gloves for cleaning, avoid picking, and book a top-coat refresh if the shine fades. Nail art aftercare tips.

What are the warning signs of a nail infection?

Redness, swelling, warmth or pain around the nail, or a nail that lifts or changes colour. Keep the skin around your nails intact, never cut cuticles harshly, and see a professional if something feels off. A simple safety guide.
