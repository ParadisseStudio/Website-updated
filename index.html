How to Take Care of Your Nails at Home: A Complete Beginner's Guide

By Angely · May 20, 2026 · 3 min read

Hi love, it's Angely. After years of sitting across the table from clients here in Richmond Hill, the question I hear most often is the simplest one: “How do I actually take care of my nails at home?” And honestly? It's a wonderful question, because the small things you do between appointments matter more than almost anything else.

Beautiful nails aren't about expensive products or hours of effort. They're about a few caring habits, repeated kindly. Let me walk you through the routine I share with my own clients.

Start with clean, gentle basics

Healthy nails begin with how you treat them every day. Wash with a mild soap, dry your hands fully (trapped moisture weakens the nail), and avoid using your nails as little tools to peel stickers or pop lids — I know, we all do it!

  • File in one direction with a soft-grit file, never a sawing motion.
  • Keep nails at a length that feels comfortable for your daily life.
  • Wear gloves for dishes and cleaning to protect them from harsh water and chemicals.

Hydration is everything

If I could give you only one tip, it would be this: moisturize your nails and cuticles daily. Dry nails crack, peel, and snap. A little cuticle oil massaged in each night, plus hand cream after washing, transforms nails over a few weeks. Think of it as watering a plant — consistency is the magic.

Feed your nails from the inside

Your nails are a reflection of your overall health. Drinking enough water, eating protein, and getting nutrients like biotin and iron all show up at your fingertips eventually. You can't rush nail growth, but you can support it.

Know when to let a professional help

At-home care keeps your nails happy day to day, but a regular professional manicure protects the cuticle, shapes the nail safely, and catches little issues early. If you'd like a hand, you can always book a manicure with us — we'd love to look after you.

Be patient with yourself and your nails. Healthy hands are built one gentle habit at a time, and you have everything you need to start today.

How to Make Your Gel Manicure Last Longer (Up to 3 Weeks)

By Angely · May 9, 2026 · 3 min read

There's a special kind of joy in a fresh gel manicure — that glassy shine, the way it makes your hands feel pulled-together. And there's a special kind of heartbreak when it chips three days later. I'm Angely, and I promise you, a little aftercare goes a long way.

It starts before the polish

A long-lasting gel manicure is built on great prep. When you come in, we gently buff, clean, and dehydrate the nail so the gel bonds properly. At home, the equivalent rule is simple: never apply oils or lotion right before a gel appointment — a dry, clean nail holds polish best.

Protect those edges

Most chips start at the free edge — the tip of your nail. Capping the tip during application (something we always do) seals that edge, but you can help too:

  • Wear gloves for dishes, cleaning, and gardening.
  • Don't use your nails as tools — open cans and packages with something else.
  • Avoid long, hot soaks in the bath, which loosen the gel over time.

Oil, oil, oil

It feels counterintuitive, but cuticle oil actually helps gel last. It keeps the surrounding skin flexible and healthy so the gel doesn't lift at the edges. A drop on each nail every night keeps everything supple and shining.

Resist the urge to pick

When a corner starts to lift, picking it off takes layers of your natural nail with it. I see it all the time, and it genuinely hurts your nails. Instead, come in for a proper removal or a quick fix.

With good prep and gentle care, two to three weeks of flawless gel is absolutely realistic. When you're ready for your next set, we'll be here — you can book online any time.

7 Signs Your Nails Need Extra Care (and What to Do)

By Angely · April 28, 2026 · 3 min read

Your nails are quietly honest. They tell you when you're a little run down, a little dehydrated, or a little overdue for some tenderness. I'm Angely, and over the years I've learned to read these little signals. Here are the ones I want you to notice too.

1. Peeling or splitting tips

Usually a sign of dryness or too much exposure to water and chemicals. More cuticle oil, hand cream, and gloves for chores will help enormously.

2. Brittle nails that snap easily

Brittleness often points to dehydration or a need for more nourishment. Be gentle with filing and feed your nails with regular moisture.

3. White spots

Most white spots are simply minor trauma — a bump or knock weeks ago. They grow out harmlessly, so there's no need to worry.

4. Vertical ridges

Gentle vertical ridges are usually just a natural part of getting older. A buffing block smooths their appearance beautifully.

5. Hangnails

These little tears at the side of the nail mean the surrounding skin is dry. Never bite or pull them — snip cleanly and moisturize.

6. Dull, lifeless color

Sometimes this just means your nails miss a little air and hydration. A polish break and some oil can bring back their natural glow.

7. Slow growth

Nails grow slower when we're stressed, unwell, or low on key nutrients. Rest, water, and good food all show up at your fingertips eventually.

If something feels off or painful, it's always worth checking in with a professional. And if you simply want a little extra care, that's exactly what we're here for.

The Ultimate Cuticle Care Routine for Healthy, Beautiful Nails

By Angely · April 17, 2026 · 3 min read

If your nails are a garden, your cuticles are the little fence that keeps everything safe. I'm Angely, and I wish more people knew how important these tiny pieces of skin truly are. Cared for kindly, they reward you with healthier, prettier nails.

What cuticles actually do

Your cuticle seals the space between your nail and your skin, keeping out bacteria and moisture that could cause infection. That's why I never recommend aggressively cutting them at home — they're protecting you.

The gentle daily routine

Caring for cuticles is soothing, almost meditative. Here's the rhythm I love:

  • After a warm shower, when the skin is soft, gently push the cuticle back with a wooden stick — never force it.
  • Massage a drop of cuticle oil into the base of each nail.
  • Follow with a rich hand cream, working it into the skin.

Done nightly, this takes two minutes and makes a visible difference within a couple of weeks.

Why oil matters so much

Cuticle oil isn't a luxury — it's the secret behind every set of healthy nails I see. It keeps the skin flexible, prevents painful hangnails, and helps your natural nail grow strong and smooth.

Leave the trimming to the professionals

When cuticles need real tidying, that's a job for a trained hand and sterile tools. A professional manicure does it safely and beautifully — we'd be glad to take care of you.

Be tender with your cuticles, and your whole hand will thank you. Small care, big difference.

How to Strengthen Weak, Brittle Nails Naturally

By Angely · April 6, 2026 · 3 min read

Few things are more frustrating than nails that finally start to grow, then snap right off. I'm Angely, and I've held a lot of hands through this exact frustration. The good news: weak nails can almost always be coaxed back to strength with patience and care.

First, understand why nails get weak

Brittle nails usually come down to one of a few things — too much water exposure, harsh products, over-filing, low moisture, or sometimes nutrition. Once you remove what's hurting them and add what nourishes them, they recover beautifully.

Protect them from water and chemicals

Water swells the nail and then leaves it dry as it evaporates, weakening it over time. Wear gloves for dishes and cleaning, and your nails will be noticeably stronger within a month.

Moisturize like it's your job

  • Cuticle oil every single night, massaged into the nail and surrounding skin.
  • Hand cream after every wash.
  • A weekly oil soak if your nails are especially thirsty.

Be gentle when you file and shape

Always file in one direction with a soft file, and skip metal tools that can split the nail. A little restraint here protects the layers of your nail.

Nourish from within

Protein, water, iron, and biotin all support stronger nails over time. You won't see overnight magic — nails grow slowly — but stay consistent and the new growth will come in noticeably tougher.

Give it six to eight weeks of gentle care and watch the difference. Your nails want to be strong — they just need you on their side.

Gel vs. Regular Polish: Which Is Better for Your Nails?

By Angely · March 26, 2026 · 3 min read

“Angely, should I get gel or regular?” I answer this question every day, and the truth is: there's no single right answer — only the right answer for you. Let me give you the honest version.

Gel polish: the long-lasting favorite

Gel is cured under LED light, so it dries instantly and stays glossy and chip-free for two to three weeks. If you want durability and shine that survives a busy life, gel is wonderful.

  • Pros: Long-lasting, high-shine, no smudging, no drying time.
  • Things to know: Needs proper removal — never peel it off, as that damages the nail.

Regular polish: the breathable classic

Regular lacquer is gentle, removes in seconds with remover, and lets you change colors on a whim. It won't last as long, but it's lovely for special occasions or when your nails want a break.

  • Pros: Easy to remove, kind to the nail, endlessly changeable.
  • Things to know: Chips sooner and needs drying time.

So which should you choose?

If your days are full and you want set-it-and-forget-it polish, go gel. If you love switching colors often or want a softer option, regular is perfect. Many of my clients alternate — gel for everyday, a regular-polish break in between.

The real secret

Honestly? Either is healthy for your nails as long as it's applied and removed properly. The damage people blame on gel almost always comes from peeling it off at home. Whichever you choose, let a professional remove it gently. You can book either with us any time.

How to Prevent Nail Breakage in Winter

By Angely · March 15, 2026 · 3 min read

Living here in Ontario, I watch it happen every year: the temperature drops, the heaters come on, and suddenly everyone's nails are peeling and snapping. I'm Angely, and winter nail care is close to my heart, because cold, dry air is a nail's biggest seasonal challenge.

Why winter is so hard on nails

Cold air outside and dry, heated air inside pull moisture straight out of your nails and the skin around them. Add hot showers and frequent hand-washing, and nails become brittle fast.

Hydrate more than you think you need to

In winter, double down on moisture:

  • Cuticle oil morning and night, not just once a day.
  • A thick hand cream you actually enjoy using — keep one by every sink.
  • An overnight treatment: cream plus cotton gloves while you sleep, once a week.

Protect your hands from the elements

Wear gloves outside — truly, every time. Cold wind is harsh on nails and skin, and warm mittens are the simplest protection there is.

Mind the hot water

I know a hot shower feels heavenly in January, but very hot water dehydrates nails. Keep it warm rather than scalding, and always moisturize the moment you step out.

Keep your length sensible

Winter is a lovely time to keep nails a touch shorter and rounded — there's simply less to catch and snap. Come spring, you can grow them out from a strong, healthy base.

Treat your nails tenderly through the cold months and they'll come out the other side happy and whole. Stay warm out there.

Why Your Nails Peel (and How to Fix It for Good)

By Angely · March 4, 2026 · 3 min read

Peeling nails drive my clients a little crazy, and I understand completely — just as they start to grow, the tips flake away in thin layers. I'm Angely, and I want to reassure you: this is one of the most fixable nail problems there is.

What peeling really is

Your nail is made of many thin layers pressed together. When those layers separate at the tip, you get peeling. It's almost always a sign of one thing: dryness, or damage from how the nail is being treated.

The most common causes

  • Too much water exposure (dishes, swimming, long baths).
  • Harsh removers or acetone used too often.
  • Filing in a back-and-forth sawing motion.
  • Peeling off gel polish — the biggest culprit of all.
  • Simple lack of moisture.

How to fix it

The plan is gentle and consistent. Stop anything that's tearing at the layers, then flood your nails with moisture: cuticle oil nightly and hand cream throughout the day. File in one direction only, and wear gloves for chores.

Give it time

Here's the part that requires patience: the peeling tips you have now are already damaged and will grow out over a couple of months. What you're really doing is making sure the new growth comes in healthy and intact. Don't be discouraged — look at the base of your nails for proof it's working.

How to Safely Remove Gel Polish Without Damaging Your Nails

By Angely · January 8, 2026 · 3 min read

Why peeling is so harmful

Gel bonds to the top layer of your nail. When you peel it, you tear those layers away, leaving your nails thin, weak, and white. That damage takes months to grow out — all from a few seconds of picking.

The safe at-home method

If you can't get to the salon, here's how to do it gently:

  • Lightly buff the shiny top layer of the gel (just the surface).
  • Soak a small cotton pad in pure acetone, place it on the nail, and wrap each finger in foil.

  • If it resists, re-soak. Patience protects your nails.

Aftercare is non-negotiable

Acetone is drying, so the moment the gel is off, flood your nails with cuticle oil and hand cream. Give them a day or two to breathe before reapplying anything.

Honestly, professional removal is faster, safer, and kinder to your nails — and it's a lovely little reset for your hands. We're always happy to remove your gel gently and prep your nails for whatever comes next.

Almost every nail infection traces back to one thing: damaged skin around the nail. Treat that little barrier kindly, keep things clean, and you'll rarely have a problem.

Healthy nails are safe nails — and you deserve both.

What cuticle oil actually does

Cuticle oil hydrates and nourishes the skin around your nail and the nail itself. That hydration keeps everything flexible, healthy, and resilient — the opposite of the dryness that causes so much nail trouble.

The benefits, one by one

  • Prevents hangnails: soft, supple skin doesn't tear and split.
  • Strengthens nails: flexible nails bend instead of snapping.
  • Supports growth: a healthy nail bed grows healthier nail.
  • Makes polish last: conditioned skin and nails resist lifting and chipping.
  • Looks gorgeous: oiled nails simply glow.

How to use it

It couldn't be simpler. Place a drop on each nail at the base, then massage it into the nail and surrounding skin. The massage matters — it works the oil in and boosts circulation. Do it every night, like brushing your teeth.

When to apply

Nighttime is ideal, so it can absorb while you sleep. But honestly, any time your hands feel dry is a good time. Keep a bottle on your nightstand, in your bag, and at your desk so you never have an excuse.

Give it three weeks

Commit to nightly oil for three weeks and you'll genuinely see a difference — softer skin, stronger nails, fewer hangnails. It's the smallest habit with the biggest reward.

One little bottle, one little moment each night. That's the not-so-secret secret to beautiful nails.

Two to three months before

One month before

How to Build a Weekly Nail Care Routine That Actually Works

By Angely · July 5, 2025 · 3 min read

Most people don't have bad nails — they have inconsistent habits. I'm Angely, and I've found that a simple, realistic routine beats sporadic bursts of effort every time. Here's a weekly rhythm you can actually stick to.

Every day

Two tiny habits carry the whole routine:

  • Cuticle oil at night — a drop per nail, massaged in.
  • Hand cream after washing — every time, all the way to your fingertips.

Twice a week

Check your shape and gently file any snags in one direction. This prevents the catch-and-tear breakage that ruins length.

Once a week

  • Push cuticles back gently after a shower.
  • Lightly buff if needed (no more than once a week).
  • Refresh your top coat if you wear regular polish.
  • Do a deeper hand treatment: cream plus cotton gloves overnight.

Every few weeks

Visit your favorite nail tech (hi!) for shaping, cuticle care, and either a fresh set of gel or a polish change. A professional touch every two to three weeks keeps everything on track and catches little issues early.

Always

Wear gloves for chores, never use your nails as tools, and never peel off your polish. These three rules prevent the vast majority of nail damage.

Keep it kind, keep it simple

Don't aim for perfect — aim for consistent. A few small, caring habits, repeated week after week, will give you the healthiest nails of your life. Start tonight with a single drop of cuticle oil, and let the routine grow from there.
